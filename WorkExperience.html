Things I learned about Computer Science & Architectural Design

Many of the skills I picked up before AI-assisted development.
  • Break Down Questions, and Find Answers in Codebase: Codebase is the most reliable source of truth. When I have a technical question, but I do not know how to find the answer from code. That is most likely because my question is not specific or small enough. I need to break down the question into smaller, more manageable parts that can be answered by examining the code. A non-specific question would be like "How does this feature work?" A more specific question would be like "Where is the entry point of this feature?", "What are the key data structures involved?", "What is the input and output?", "What issue does this feature solve?"

Reflections on Communication

  • Closing Communication Gaps: After I asked my peers or mentees to do some work, I normally ask them to put into their own words what they understood from my instructions. This helps identify any misunderstandings early and ensures everyone is on the same page. Similarly, after getting instructions from managers or seniors, I normally put "my understanding is XXX". This way, if I misunderstood something, it can be corrected early.
  • Information Density: "State important things three times" does not literally mean repeating the same words three times. It means giving three times of the information density, like drawing Component Diagram, State Machine Diagram, and Sequence Diagram to convey the same concept from different perspectives. If drawing Component Diagram three times, the mis-interpretation still exists there. But with three different diagrams, a mis-interpretation will naturally cause people to wonder why the information does not align, and hence prompt clarification.
  • Extreme Ownership: I found extreme ownership gives me freedom. I can reject workload that is beyond me. But for tasks I committed, extreme ownership means not limiting myself to the boundaries of my formal role. It requires me to practively follow up other team's progress, organize alignment meeting to get everyone on the same page, push initiatives forward even when I am not the official lead, and try different approaches to eventually solve the problem.
  • Reduce Friction in Decision Making: Humans are often the slowest point in any process. Two major bottlenecks are communication and decision-making. I try to anticipate the information and context stakeholders need in order to make decisions efficiently. When critical context is missing, stakeholders are more likely to escalate decisions upward for additional approval, which slows down execution and delays progress.
